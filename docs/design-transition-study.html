Signal acquired

The app has four states and they arrive like four different screens. This is what actually moves between them — measured off the running build, not remembered — and a playable proposal for making the whole run feel like one instrument gaining signal.

Both readings are right, and the second one has a cause that is closer to a defect than to a taste call. Here is the run frame by frame.

Five things move, and only one of them is animation

cause 01 The reveal blanks the stage Each element enters with backwards fill and a stagger delay, so during its delay it holds opacity 0 — including the name and count that were already on screen a frame earlier.
cause 02 The message slot resizes Two lines idle, one starting, three waiting, none capturing. Two lines are reserved, so waiting overflows the reserve and pushes the button 12 px down.
cause 03 The status dot travels The line is centred, so the width of the words drags the dot sideways — 54 px across the four states. The one element the eye holds onto never stays still.
cause 04 Nothing fades, ever Every string is a bare assignment. Four labels, four messages, two chip readings and the button caption all hard-cut, several of them in the same frame.
cause 05 Everything lands at once Press Start and the button, the status line, the counter reset and the message all change on the same tick. Simultaneous change reads as a flash; a chain reads as cause and effect.
not a cause The rain is already right It fades from half to full strength over 900 ms and reads well. Its one gap is speed: the drift snaps from stopped to full. Easing that in is the cheapest win on this page.

The idea: one instrument, gaining signal

The app is a listener. Idle it is off; starting it powers up; waiting it is listening and hearing nothing; capturing it has the signal. That is one continuous process, and the interface should read as one object passing through it — not as four screens taking turns.

Which gives a rule strict enough to design against:

Nothing in the greeting may ever change the layout. Every slot is reserved at its worst case across all six languages. State changes are expressed in ink, colour, glow and motion of the same elements — never by something appearing where nothing was, and never by something leaving a hole.

Once that holds, “no jumps” is a property of the structure rather than something the animation has to paper over. The animation is then free to do the interesting part: make the arrival of a signal feel like an arrival.

Three moves worth the effort

  • The dot becomes the instrument. Amber with a rotating sweep while the engine starts; amber with a slow radar ping while it listens; green and breathing once traffic lands. It stops sliding sideways — the words glide to their new width underneath it instead.
  • The reveal becomes a wave, not a rebuild. A brightness-and-scale pulse starting at the dot and passing up through name, count, caption and chips over ~600 ms. Everything stays visible throughout; energy travels through the card.
  • The count rolls. Digits rise into place instead of snapping, and the session reset falls to zero rather than blinking to it. It is the largest thing on the screen and currently the least alive.

Can the dot pulse on real loot?

Yes — and asking the question found something worth fixing on its own. Here is what actually reaches the app today, read out of the running code:

  • The engine prints a heartbeat roughly every 60 seconds: [status] character: Bors · lines written: 4. That is where the counter's number comes from.
  • A 5-second file poll exists as a fallback — but it is ignored once a heartbeat has been parsed (if (!heartbeatSeen)), because the engine's own count is the more trustworthy of the two.
  • So on a healthy run the count you are watching can be up to a minute stale. Your instinct was right, and it is worse than “every X seconds”.
  • But the engine also prints one line per pickup, the moment it happens: 05:42:00 UTC: {UA} [VITRYLA] Bors looted 1x Expert's Rune from @MOB_MORGANA… — it is in our recorded fixture, and the adapter currently classifies it as noise.

So the live signal is already arriving on the wire we already read; we throw it away. Parsing it gives two things at once: the dot can pulse on the real event, and the counter can move when loot lands instead of a minute later — which is the part I would ship even if you hated the pulse.

Two guard rails. The log file stays the source of truth for everything uploaded — this line is only used to count and to flash, so a mis-parse can never corrupt what officers settle from. And it gets a strict pattern with a validated capture, not a loose one: the lesson from the heartbeat that parsed a character called “not” is that a liberal pattern fails by matching. The heartbeat's number still wins whenever it arrives, as the reconciling truth.

Timing, as three tokens

--t-ink 140ms for colour and opacity · --t-swap 220ms for a text cross-fade · --t-beat 700ms for the capture wave, plus a --t-ramp 1200ms for the glows and the rain. Everything picks one of the four; under prefers-reduced-motion they all collapse to zero and the end states stay exactly as they are.

What this touches

The renderer, the stylesheet, and one string per language. No change to the state machine, the engine adapter, the supervisor or the uploader — the four states and their transitions are already right; only their presentation changes. Concretely: a swapText helper and a count roller in renderer.ts, the fixed-stage rules and the wave in styles.css, a shorter waiting sentence in strings.ts ×6, and the rain module gains a speed ramp.

The one thing I would not do is animate the height of the message slot. Reserving it costs a few pixels of empty space in the short states and buys a card that cannot move; animating it keeps the movement and adds latency to it.

Settled

Your answers, and what each one means in the build.

Shorter waiting copy agreed

“Listening. No Albion traffic yet — it is picked up the moment the game sends any.” Two lines in all six languages; the restart reassurance moves to the hints that open after 90 s of quiet. This is the single biggest jump in the run, and it goes away.

A line while capturing agreed

“Loot near MockWarrior is being written to the log.” The message slot is now never blank in any state, which is what lets it be a fixed height without looking broken.

The count rolls, within reason agreed

One roll per step while the jump is small; several lines landing at once set the number outright. No slot machine in a ZvZ.

The pulse on real loot answered above

The live line is already arriving and being discarded. Parsing it makes the pulse possible and takes the counter's lag from a minute to instant. I would ship the counter fix regardless and put the pulse on top of it.

The pill morph and the text reworked

Both were too quick. Text now leaves in 170 ms and arrives in 260, travelling 7 px as it goes, and each element starts 50 ms after the one before it. The pill drains rather than cross-fades, over 440 ms, with a slight squash at the midpoint. Judge it on the pill bench — that is the control you press most.
